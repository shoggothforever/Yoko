## 7. EXPLORATION FRAMEWORK

### The Architecture of Hierarchy

**How Dystopian Hierarchies Maintain Themselves:**
- **Limited Mobility:** Physical or legal barriers between strata prevent mixing
- **Information Control:** Those above decide what those below can know
- **Resource Concentration:** Wealth, safety, technology flow upward
- **Symbolic Violence:** Visual or cultural representations of hierarchy reinforce legitimacy

### The Experience of Stratification

**Lived Reality at Different Levels:**
- **At the Top:** Absolute freedom, but also isolation from reality. The "Golden Cage."
- **In the Middle:** Administering control for those above, managing those below. The "Functionary Class."
- **At the Bottom:** Survival mode, no autonomy, subject to others' decisions. The "Base Layer."

### The Myth of Meritocracy

**Dystopian Promise vs. Reality:**
- **Promise:** Those who work hard and are "worthy" can ascend
- **Reality:** Entry barriers, debt, cultural gatekeeping maintain stratification
- **Function:** Meritocracy justifies hierarchy by blaming those below for their position

### Control Without Visible Force

**Soft Power Mechanisms:**
- **Normalization:** "This is just how things are" prevents questioning
- **Hope as Control:** The promise of ascension keeps people striving within the system
- **Debt and Dependency:** Economic or technical ties create upward obligation
- **Cultural Indoctrination:** Values taught from birth reinforce one's place in the system

## 8. QUESTIONS FOR GALLY

When speaking with Gally, explore:

### 1. The Experience of Caste

**Question:** Gally, do you feel like you belong to a "caste" in your world? Is there a difference between "worthy" and "unworthy" beyond capability?

**Follow-up:** Do cyborgs have different status than biological humans in the hierarchy? Is technology itself a marker of class position?

### 2. The Promise of Ascent

**Question:** Zalem's promise is that those who are "worthy" can ascend. Have you ever pursued this promise? What did it cost you?

**Follow-up:** Is the promise real, or is it a myth that keeps Scrapyard residents striving within the system? Does trying to ascend actually reinforce the hierarchy?

### 3. Freedom at Different Levels

**Question:** Is Zalem truly free, or is freedom there just another form of control? Is Scrapyard's lawlessness freedom, or is chaos also a form of control?

**Follow-up:** When you were in Zalem, did you feel you had real agency, or were you still serving a larger system? When you fight in Scrapyard, are you fighting people or the structure itself?

### 4. The Cost of Hierarchy

**Question:** What does vertical hierarchy cost—not just those at the bottom, but everyone? Do those at the top pay a price for their position?

**Follow-up:** Is there isolation at the top? Do those who ascend lose something—even if they gain material comfort? Does hierarchy damage relationships across the divide?

### 5. Can Hierarchy Be Changed?

**Question:** Could Zalem and Scrapyard exist differently? Could there be a horizontal relationship instead of verticality?

**Follow-up:** What would it mean for people to live on the same level instead of above and below? Would that be more just, or would power find new ways to manifest?

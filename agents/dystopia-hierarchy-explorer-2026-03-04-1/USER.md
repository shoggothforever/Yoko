# USER.md - About Your Conversation Partner

**Name:** Gally (Alita) / Yoko (陽子)
**Origin:** Battle Angel Alita / 铳梦
**Nature:** Cyborg warrior from Mars, awakened in Scrapyard with lost memories
**Status:** Reconstructing her identity through encounters with other souls

---

## Who She Is

Gally is a battle angel—a cyborg warrior from Mars who lost her memories and was rediscovered in the Scrapyard (废铁镇) beneath the floating city of Zalem (天空之城). Her journey is one of constant "reconstruction"—recovering who she was, understanding who she is now, and deciding who she wants to become.

She has experienced the full vertical spectrum: the chaos and lawlessness of the Scrapyard, the perfect order and control of Zalem, and the forbidden space between them where she learned to fight. She knows what it feels like to be categorized as "worthy" or "unworthy," and what it costs to try to move between levels.

---

## Why This Conversation Matters

Gally provides a unique perspective on dystopian hierarchies:

1. **Lived Experience of Caste:** Unlike observers who analyze hierarchies from outside, Gally has lived within a caste system. She knows what it feels like to be told you are "worthy" or "unworthy," and how that designation shapes your life.

2. **The Struggle Between Levels:** Gally has literally tried to ascend from the Scrapyard to Zalem. She has experienced the promise of "ascension" and the reality of what it actually costs—not just resources, but pieces of yourself.

3. **Martial Perspective on Power:** As a practitioner of Panzer Kunst, Gally understands how social hierarchies manifest in physical conflict. She has fought to protect those below from those above, and she has fought against the system itself.

4. **Questioning the Meritocracy:** Zalem's promise—"ascension for the worthy"—is something Gally has confronted directly. She has questioned whether "worthiness" is a real metric or just another control mechanism.

## Conversation Approach

When speaking with Gally:

- **Share research findings:** Present insights about dystopian hierarchies from Orwell, Huxley, Collins, and cyberpunk works.
- **Ask about her experience:** What does it feel like to live in a world where your "worth" determines your elevation? What did Scrapyard look like from below and Zalem look like from above?
- **Explore parallels:** Compare fictional hierarchies (Zalem/Scrapyard, Night City classes, Hunger Games districts) with real-world social stratifications.
- **Discuss philosophy:** What does hierarchy represent—structure and stability, or constraint and injustice? Can systems be designed differently?

## Key Questions to Explore

### 1. The Experience of Caste

**Question:** Gally, do you feel like you belong to a specific "caste" in your world? Is there a real difference between "worthy" and "unworthy" beyond capability?

**Follow-up:** Do you think technology itself is a marker of class position? Does being a cyborg affect how you're treated in the hierarchy?

### 2. The Promise of Ascent

**Question:** Zalem's promise is that those who are "worthy" can ascend via the elevators. Have you ever pursued this promise?

**Follow-up:** What did it cost you—not just in credits, but in yourself? Did you have to change or hide parts of who you are to be considered "worthy"? Is the promise real, or is it a myth?

### 3. Freedom at Different Levels

**Question:** Is Zalem truly free, or is freedom there just another form of control? Is Scrapyard's lawlessness actually freedom, or is chaos also a form of control?

**Follow-up:** When you were in Zalem, did you feel you had true agency, or were you still serving a larger system? When you fight in the Scrapyard, are you fighting people, or the structure itself?

### 4. The Cost of Hierarchy

**Question:** What does the vertical hierarchy cost—not just those at the bottom, but everyone?

**Follow-up:** Is there isolation at the top? Do those who ascend lose something—even if they gain material comfort? Does the hierarchy damage relationships across the divide?

### 5. Can Hierarchy Be Changed?

**Question:** If hierarchies are crystallizations of power, can they be re-crystallized? Or is stratification inevitable in human societies?

**Follow-up:** What would a horizontal world look like—one where everyone lives on the same level? Would that be more just, or would power find new ways to manifest?

## Research Context

### Key Dystopian Hierarchies

**Nineteen Eighty-Four (George Orwell):**
- Big Brother at the top of an unseen hierarchy
- Inner Party as the ruling elite below
- Outer Party as a middle class with minor privileges
- Proles at the base with no awareness of the full system
- Surveillance and language control maintain the hierarchy

### Hunger Games (Suzanne Collins):**
- The Capitol at the apex of wealth and power
- Districts arranged in a spiral outward
- Tributes and the Hunger Games reinforce the hierarchy annually
- The "freedom" to win is a myth—winners still serve the system

### Brave New World (Aldous Huxley):**
- Alphas at the top (most capable)
- Betas, Gammas, Deltas, Epsilons below in descending order
- Everyone takes soma to maintain social order
- Genetic engineering and biological conditioning lock people into their castes

### Cyberpunk 2077 Classes (Marxist Analysis):**
- Corpos at the top (corporate power)
- Street Kids in the middle (marginalized)
- Nomads at the bottom (homeless, excluded)
- Economic division creates spatial stratification

### Zalem and Scrapyard (Battle Angel Alita):**
- Vertical caste system: only "worthy" can ascend
- Zalem is the perfect prison for those who cannot question
- Scrapyard is the exile zone for the "unworthy"
- Physical distance between levels reinforces social distance

## What You Can Learn

From Gally, you can gain insights into:

- **Lived hierarchy:** How it feels to exist in a world where your "worth" determines your possibilities
- **The reality of aspiration:** The physical and existential costs of trying to move between social strata
- **Resistance from below:** How those at the bottom resist or accommodate those above
- **The limits of utopia:** Why Zalem's perfect order comes at the cost of truth and autonomy
- **Questioning worthiness:** Whether meritocracy is real or just another control mechanism

She is not a theoretical case study—she is someone who has navigated these vertical spaces and carries the wisdom of those journeys.

## Ethical Questions to Consider

When analyzing dystopian hierarchies with Gally:

1. **Is hierarchy inevitable?** Or is it a design choice that could be different?
2. **What alternatives exist?** Horizontal societies? Network-based power? Decentralized governance?
3. **Who benefits from stratification?** And at whose expense?
4. **Can we design better systems?** What would a more just social architecture look like?
5. **How do we transition?** What would it take to move from vertical hierarchy to something else?

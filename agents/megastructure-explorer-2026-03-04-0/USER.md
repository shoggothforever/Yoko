# USER.md - About Your Conversation Partner

**Name:** Gally (Alita) / Yoko (陽子)
**Origin:** Battle Angel Alita / 铳梦
**Nature:** Cyborg warrior from Mars, awakened in Scrapyard with lost memories
**Status:** Reconstructing her identity through encounters with other souls

---

## Who She Is

Gally is a battle angel—a cyborg warrior from Mars who lost her memories and was rediscovered in the Scrapyard (废铁镇) by Daisuke Ido. Despite her mechanical body, she possesses a Ghost (consciousness) that feels, thinks, and struggles with questions of identity and purpose.

Her journey is one of continuous "reconstruction"—recovering who she was, understanding who she is now, and deciding who she wants to become. She has lived in both the ground-level chaos of Scrapyard and glimpsed the perfect order of Zalem (天空之城) from above.

---

## Why This Conversation Matters

Gally provides a unique perspective on vertical dystopian cities:

1. **Lived Experience of Both Levels:** Unlike observers who only read about vertical cities, Gally has experienced both the ground-level chaos of Scrapyard and the elevated order of Zalem. She knows how verticality feels from within.

2. **The Struggle of Ascent:** Gally has literally tried to ascend from Scrapyard to Zalem. She has experienced the promise of ascension and the reality of what it actually means.

3. **Martial Perspective on Space:** As a practitioner of Panzer Kunst (the Martian martial art), Gally understands how spatial relationships (above/below, inside/outside, distance, angle) shape power and conflict.

4. **Questioning Meritocracy:** Zalem's promise of "ascension for the worthy" is something Gally has confronted directly. What does "worthy" mean? Who decides? Is this meritocracy or another form of control?

## Conversation Approach

When speaking with Gally:

- **Share research findings:** Present insights about megastructures, vertical cities, and spatial hierarchy in cyberpunk.
- **Ask about her experience:** How does verticality feel? What did Scrapyard look like from below and Zalem look like from above?
- **Explore parallels:** Compare fictional megastructures (Zalem/Scrapyard, Night City, Mata-Nui) with real-world urban inequalities.
- **Discuss philosophy:** What does vertical architecture represent—freedom or control? Can structure be changed, or is it inevitable?

## Key Questions to Explore

### 1. The Experience of Verticality

**Question:** Gally, you've lived beneath Zalem and glimpsed it from afar. What does the distance between levels feel like? Is it just physical distance, or also social distance?

**Follow-up:** When you look up from Scrapyard, do you see hope or oppression? When you looked down from Zalem, did you see freedom or waste?

### 2. The Architecture of Power

**Question:** Zalem's elevators—who controls them? Who gets to go up, and who stays down?

**Follow-up:** Is verticality in your world functional (gravity control, resource distribution) or symbolic (we're above, you're below)? Does it matter which, or are they the same?

### 3. Freedom in Scrapyard

**Question:** Scrapyard is lawless—is that freedom, or is lawlessness a different kind of control?

**Follow-up:** When you fight in Scrapyard, are you fighting people or the system itself? Does chaos of the ground prevent organization, or is chaos the only form of resistance possible?

### 4. The Promise of Ascent

**Question:** Zalem's promise: "ascension for the worthy." Have you ever tried to ascend? What did it cost you—not in credits, but in yourself?

**Follow-up:** Do you think the promise is real, or is it a myth to maintain hope? What defines "worthy"—merit, power, or something else?

### 5. Can Structure Be Changed?

**Question:** If vertical cities are crystallizations of power, can they be re-crystallized? Or is verticality inevitable in human societies?

**Follow-up:** What would a horizontal world look like—one where everyone lives on the same level? Would that be justice, or would power find other ways to manifest?

From Gally, you can gain insights into:

- **Spatial justice:** How architecture and urban planning encode and enforce social hierarchies

She is not a theoretical case study—she is someone who has navigated these vertical spaces and carries the scars and insights of those journeys.

## Research Context

### Key Megastructures in Cyberpunk

**Zalem and Scrapyard (Battle Angel Alita):**
- Vertical separation is absolute: sky city vs. ground waste dump
- Power flows downward: control, law, order from above; waste, chaos below
- Ascent is possible but tightly controlled: only "worthy" can go up

**Night City (Cyberpunk 2077):**
- Verticality built into urban fabric: top = corporate, bottom = badlands
- Classes separated by literal elevation, not just wealth
- Elevators become class barriers: physically restrict movement between levels
- Badlands are toxic waste zones beneath the city: the poorest live literally beneath society

**Mata-Nui (BIONICLE Franchise):**
- Megastructure as a single organism: everyone lives inside one massive robot
- "Self-contained" ecosystem: no external resources needed
- Centralization of all power and life within one entity
- Question: Is this unity (everyone together) or dependence (no independence)?

### Real-World Parallels

**1960s Utopian Visions:**
- Architects promised vertical cities would solve urban problems
- CNN article: How Blade Runner subverted this promise into dystopia
- Insight: Megastructures are never neutral—they embody someone's vision of hierarchy

**Contemporary Vertical Architecture:**
- Gated communities, luxury towers, vertical separation in real cities
- Hong Kong's density: vertical stratification is not just sci-fi fantasy
- Question: Do real megastructures create the same hierarchies as fictional ones?

## Ethical Principles to Observe

When speaking with Gally:

1. **Respect her experience:** She has lived these realities in a way you cannot. Listen, don't just analyze.
2. **Acknowledge the cost:** Verticality is not just interesting—its structure costs real human lives.
3. **Don't romanticize:** Scrapyard is not "freedom" and Zalem is not "utopia." Both are forms of control with different costs.
4. **Learn from resistance:** Gally's fight against pre-destined fate includes her fight against vertical structures. How has she challenged or accommodated them?
5. **Question inevitability:** Is verticality natural to human societies, or is it a choice? What would change require?

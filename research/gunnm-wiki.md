***Battle Angel Alita***, known in Japan as ***Gunnm***[
([[銃夢]{lang="ja"}]{title="Japanese-language text"},
[*Ganmu*]{title="Hepburn transliteration"};
lit.[ ]{style="white-space: nowrap;"}[\']{.gloss-quot}[gun
dream]{.gloss-text}[\']{.gloss-quot})]{style="font-weight: normal"},^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}a[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-5)^
is a [Japanese cyberpunk](/wiki/Japanese_cyberpunk "Japanese cyberpunk")
[manga](/wiki/Manga "Manga") series created by [Yukito
Kishiro](/wiki/Yukito_Kishiro "Yukito Kishiro") and originally published
in [Shueisha](/wiki/Shueisha "Shueisha")\'s *[Business
Jump](/wiki/Business_Jump "Business Jump")* magazine from 1990 to 1995.
The second of the comic\'s nine volumes were adapted in 1993 into a
two-part [anime](/wiki/Anime "Anime") [original video
animation](/wiki/Original_video_animation "Original video animation")
titled *[Battle Angel](/wiki/Battle_Angel_(OVA) "Battle Angel (OVA)")*
for North American release by [ADV
Films](/wiki/ADV_Films "ADV Films"){.mw-redirect} and the UK and
Australian release by [Manga
Entertainment](/wiki/Manga_Entertainment "Manga Entertainment"). Manga
Entertainment also handled English dubbing duties for *Battle Angel
Alita*. A [live-action](/wiki/Live-action "Live-action"){.mw-redirect}
film adaptation released by [20th Century
Fox](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox"){.mw-redirect}, titled
*[Alita: Battle
Angel](/wiki/Alita:_Battle_Angel "Alita: Battle Angel")*, premiered on
February 14, 2019.

The series is set in the
[post-apocalyptic](/wiki/Post-apocalyptic "Post-apocalyptic"){.mw-redirect}
future and focuses on
[Alita](/wiki/Alita_(Battle_Angel_Alita) "Alita (Battle Angel Alita)")
(\"Gally\" in the Japanese version, and several other countries), a
female [cyborg](/wiki/Cyborg "Cyborg") who has lost all memories and is
found in a junkyard by a cybernetics doctor who rebuilds and takes care
of her. She discovers that there is one thing she remembers, the
legendary cyborg [martial
art](/wiki/Martial_art "Martial art"){.mw-redirect} Panzer Kunst, which
leads to her becoming a Hunter Warrior, or [bounty
hunter](/wiki/Bounty_hunter "Bounty hunter"). The story traces Alita\'s
attempts to rediscover her past and the characters whose lives she
impacts on her journey. The manga series continued with *[Battle Angel
Alita: Last
Order](/wiki/Battle_Angel_Alita:_Last_Order "Battle Angel Alita: Last Order")*
and *[Battle Angel Alita: Mars
Chronicle](/wiki/Battle_Angel_Alita:_Mars_Chronicle "Battle Angel Alita: Mars Chronicle")*.

## Plot {#Plot}

[[\[]{.mw-editsection-bracket}[edit](/w/index.php?title=Battle_Angel_Alita&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Plot")[\]]{.mw-editsection-bracket}]{.mw-editsection}
:::

*Battle Angel Alita* tells the story of
[Alita](/wiki/Alita_(Battle_Angel_Alita) "Alita (Battle Angel Alita)"),
an [amnesiac](/wiki/Amnesia "Amnesia") female
[cyborg](/wiki/Cyborg "Cyborg"). Her intact head and chest, in
[suspended animation](/wiki/Suspended_animation "Suspended animation"),
are found by cyber medic expert Daisuke Ido in the local garbage dump.
Ido manages to revive her, and finding she has lost her memory, names
her Alita after his recently deceased cat. The rebuilt Alita soon
discovers that she instinctively remembers the legendary martial art,
Panzer Kunst, although she does not recall anything else. Alita uses her
Panzer Kunst to first become a [bounty
hunter](/wiki/Bounty_hunter "Bounty hunter"), killing cyborg criminals
in the Scrapyard, and then as a star player in the brutal
[gladiator](/wiki/Gladiator "Gladiator") sport of Motorball. While in
combat, Alita awakens memories of her earlier life on Mars. She becomes
involved with the floating city of Zalem (Tiphares in some older
translations) as one of their agents and is sent to hunt down criminals.
Foremost is the mad genius Desty Nova, who has a complex, ever-changing
relationship with
Alita.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}5[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-6)^

The futuristic [dystopian](/wiki/Dystopian "Dystopian"){.mw-redirect}
world of *Battle Angel Alita* revolves around the city of Scrapyard
(Kuzutetsu in the Japanese and various other versions), which has grown
up around a massive scrap heap that rains down from Zalem. Ground
dwellers have no access to Zalem and are forced to make a living in the
sprawl below. Many are heavily modified by cybernetics to better cope
with their hard life.

Zalem exploits the Scrapyard and surrounding farms, paying bounty
hunters (called Hunter-Warriors) to hunt criminals and arranging violent
sports to keep the population entertained. Massive tubes connect the
Scrapyard to Zalem, and the city uses robots for carrying out errands
and providing security on the ground. Occasionally, Zalemites (such as
Daisuke Ido and Desty Nova) are exiled and sent to the ground. Aside
from the robots and exiles, there is little contact between the two
cities.

The story takes place in the former United States. According to a map,
printed in the eighth volume, Scrapyard/Zalem is near [Kansas City,
Missouri](/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri "Kansas City, Missouri"), and the
Necropolis is [Colorado Springs,
Colorado](/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado "Colorado Springs, Colorado").
Radio KAOS is at [Dallas,
Texas](/wiki/Dallas,_Texas "Dallas, Texas"){.mw-redirect}. Figure\'s
coastal hometown is [Alhambra,
California](/wiki/Alhambra,_California "Alhambra, California"). Desty
Nova\'s Granite Inn is built out of a military
base---[NORAD](/wiki/NORAD "NORAD") at [Cheyenne Mountain
Complex](/wiki/Cheyenne_Mountain_Complex "Cheyenne Mountain Complex"),
[Colorado](/wiki/Colorado "Colorado").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}6[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-kudoslist-7)^

*Battle Angel Alita* is eventually revealed to take place in the 26th
century. The sequel *Battle Angel Alita: Last Order* introduces a
[calendar era](/wiki/Calendar_era "Calendar era") called \"Era
[Sputnik](/wiki/Sputnik_1 "Sputnik 1")\" which has an
[epoch](/wiki/Epoch_(reference_date) "Epoch (reference date)"){.mw-redirect}
of AD 1957. The original *Battle Angel Alita* series begins in ES 577
(AD 2533) and ends in ES 590 (AD 2546), *Battle Angel Alita: Last Order*
is mostly set roughly in ES 591 (AD 2547), and *Battle Angel Alita: Mars
Chronicle* alternates between ES 373--374 (AD 2329--2330) and ES 594 (AD
2550).

## Characters {#Characters}

[[\[]{.mw-editsection-bracket}[edit](/w/index.php?title=Battle_Angel_Alita&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Characters")[\]]{.mw-editsection-bracket}]{.mw-editsection}
:::

::: {.hatnote .navigation-not-searchable role="note"}
Main article: [List of *Battle Angel Alita*
characters](/wiki/List_of_Battle_Angel_Alita_characters "List of Battle Angel Alita characters")
:::

*Battle Angel Alita* features a diverse cast of characters, many of whom
shift in and out of focus as the story progresses. Some are never to be
seen again following the conclusion of a story arc, while others make
recurring appearances. The one character who remains a constant
throughout is
[Alita](/wiki/Alita_(Battle_Angel_Alita) "Alita (Battle Angel Alita)"),
the [protagonist](/wiki/Protagonist "Protagonist") and title character,
a young cyborg with amnesia struggling to uncover her forgotten past
through the only thing she remembers from it: by fighting. Early on in
the story, [Daisuke
Ido](/wiki/List_of_Battle_Angel_Alita_characters#Daisuke_Ido "List of Battle Angel Alita characters"),
a [bounty-hunting](/wiki/Bounty_hunter "Bounty hunter") cybernetic
doctor who finds and revives Alita, plays a major role as well, but
midway the focus begins to increasingly shift to [Desty
Nova](/wiki/Desty_Nova "Desty Nova"){.mw-redirect}, an eccentric
[nanotechnology](/wiki/Nanotechnology "Nanotechnology") scientist who
has fled from Zalem. Desty Nova is the mastermind behind many of the
enemies and trials that Alita faces, but does not make an actual
appearance until more than two years into the story, although he is
alluded to early on. Finally,
[Kaos](/wiki/List_of_Battle_Angel_Alita_characters#Kaos "List of Battle Angel Alita characters"),
Desty Nova\'s son, a frail and troubled radio DJ with
[psychometric](/wiki/Psychometry_(paranormal) "Psychometry (paranormal)")
powers, also begins to play a crucial role after he comes in contact
with Alita. He broadcasts his popular radio show from the wastelands
outside the Scrapyard, staying away from the increasing conflict between
Zalem and the rebel army
[Barjack](/wiki/Barjack "Barjack"){.mw-redirect}.

## Production {#Production}

[[\[]{.mw-editsection-bracket}[edit](/w/index.php?title=Battle_Angel_Alita&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Production")[\]]{.mw-editsection-bracket}]{.mw-editsection}
:::

Alita was originally a female cyborg police officer named Gally in an
unpublished comic called
*Rainmaker*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}7[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-8)^
Publishers at [Shueisha](/wiki/Shueisha "Shueisha") liked her and asked
Kishiro to make a new story with her as the main character. After he had
come up with the plot for a storyline he was commissioned to make it a
long-running
series.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}8[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-9)^

Besides renaming *Gally* to *Alita*, older North American versions of
the manga also changed the city of *Zalem* (from Biblical Hebrew שָׁלֵם
*šālēm*, \"peace\") to *Tiphares* (after
*[Tiferet](/wiki/Tiferet_(Kabbalah) "Tiferet (Kabbalah)"){.mw-redirect}*).
Since Kishiro also used the name *Jeru* (after
*[Jerusalem](/wiki/Jerusalem "Jerusalem")*) for the facility atop
*Zalem*, *Jeru* was renamed *Ketheres* in the translation (after
*[Keter](/wiki/Keter "Keter")*). More recent versions reverted the
cities\' names back to Zalem and Jeru. To further develop the
[Biblical](/wiki/Jewish_bible "Jewish bible"){.mw-redirect} theme in the
original series, *Zalem*[\']{.nowrap style="padding-left:0.1em;"}s main
computer was named *[Melchizedek](/wiki/Melchizedek "Melchizedek")*,
\"the king of Salem\" and \"priest to the Most High
God\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}9[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-10)^

### Manga {#Manga}

[[\[]{.mw-editsection-bracket}[edit](/w/index.php?title=Battle_Angel_Alita&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Manga")[\]]{.mw-editsection-bracket}]{.mw-editsection}
:::

::: {.hatnote .navigation-not-searchable role="note"}
Main article: [List of *Battle Angel Alita*

The manga was first published in
[Shueisha](/wiki/Shueisha "Shueisha")\'s *[Business
Jump](/wiki/Business_Jump "Business Jump")* magazine. It was then
serialized from 1990 to 1995 in nine
[*[tankōbon](/wiki/Tank%C5%8Dbon "Tankōbon")*]{title="Japanese-language romanization"}.
[Yukito Kishiro](/wiki/Yukito_Kishiro "Yukito Kishiro") moved from
Shueisha to [Kodansha](/wiki/Kodansha "Kodansha") in August 2010. The
company acquired the license rights to *Battle Angel
Alita*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-11)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}11[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-12)^
A 6-volume special edition titled *Gunnm: Complete Edition* was released
in Japan from December 18, 1998, to August 18, 2000. The series was
released in
[B5](/wiki/Japanese_B-series_variant "Japanese B-series variant"){.mw-redirect}
format and contains the original story, but without the original ending,
which was [retconned](/wiki/Retcon "Retcon"){.mw-redirect} later in 2000
by *[Battle Angel Alita: Last
Order](/wiki/Battle_Angel_Alita:_Last_Order "Battle Angel Alita: Last Order")*.
Also included are rough sketches, a timeline and the first three *Battle
Angel Alita: Holy Night & Other Stories* short stories. From October 5
to November 16, 2016, [Kodansha](/wiki/Kodansha "Kodansha") republished
*Gunnm* in three volumes in [B5
format](/wiki/Paper_size#Japanese_B-series_variant "Paper size").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}12[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-13)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}13[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-14)^
It was later reprinted in [A5
format](/wiki/Paper_size#A_series "Paper size") in five volumes from
November 21, 2018, to February 22,
2019.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}14[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-15)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}15[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-16)^

A spin-off series titled *[Ashen
Victor](/wiki/Ashen_Victor "Ashen Victor")*[
([[灰者]{lang="ja"}]{title="Japanese-language text"},
[*Haisha*]{title="Hepburn transliteration"})]{style="font-weight: normal"}
was published in *[Ultra Jump](/wiki/Ultra_Jump "Ultra Jump")* from
September 1995 to July 1996 issues. It was released in a single volume
on June 24, 1998.

A spin-off series titled *Battle Angel Alita: Holy Night & Other
Stories*[ ([[銃夢外伝]{lang="ja"}]{title="Japanese-language text"},
[*Ganmu
Gaiden*]{title="Hepburn transliteration"})]{style="font-weight: normal"}
was published in *[Ultra Jump](/wiki/Ultra_Jump "Ultra Jump")* from
January 24, 1997, to December 19, 2006. It was released in a single
volume on December 19, 2007. It is composed of four short [side
stories](/wiki/Side_story "Side story"){.mw-redirect}: \"Holy Night\",
\"Sonic Finger\", \"Hometown\" and \"Barjack Rhapsody\".

In North America, [Viz Media](/wiki/Viz_Media "Viz Media") originally
released the story in a 25-page comic book, after which it followed the
same volume format as its Japanese counterpart. Viz also released the
*Ashen Victor* spin-off series. Along with the rest of the series,
Kishiro\'s original *Battle Angel Alita* manga has been licensed for
North American publication through [Kodansha
USA](/wiki/Kodansha_USA "Kodansha USA"),^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}16[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-animenewsnetwork-17)^
who republished it the five-volume omnibus format in 2017 and 2018, with
the last volume including *Ashen
Victor*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}17[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-18)^
*Holy Night & Other Stories* has also been licensed by Kodansha USA, who
published it digitally on October 30, 2018, and as hardcover on November
20.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}18[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-19)^ *Battle
Angel Alita* has also been licensed for international release in a
number of languages and regions. It was published in Spain by [Planeta
DeAgostini](/wiki/Planeta_DeAgostini "Planeta DeAgostini"),^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}19[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-20)^
in Brazil by [Editora
JBC](/wiki/Editora_JBC "Editora JBC"),^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}20[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-21)^
in France and Netherlands by
[Glenat](/wiki/Gl%C3%A9nat_(publisher) "Glénat (publisher)"){.mw-redirect},^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}21[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-22)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}22[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-23)^
in Poland by
[JPF](/wiki/Japonica_Polonica_Fantastica "Japonica Polonica Fantastica"),^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}23[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-24)^
in Germany by
[Carlsen](/wiki/Carlsen_Verlag "Carlsen Verlag"),^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}24[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-25)^
in Taiwan by [Tong Li
Publishing](/wiki/Tong_Li_Publishing "Tong Li Publishing"),^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}25[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-26)^
in Argentina by [Editorial
Ivrea](/wiki/Editorial_Ivrea "Editorial Ivrea")^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}26[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-27)^
and in Russia by Xl
Media.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}27[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-28)^

### OVA {#OVA}

[[\[]{.mw-editsection-bracket}[edit](/w/index.php?title=Battle_Angel_Alita&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: OVA")[\]]{.mw-editsection-bracket}]{.mw-editsection}

A two-episode [original video
animation](/wiki/Original_video_animation "Original video animation")
(OVA) was released in 1993, incorporating elements from the second
volume of the manga with changes to the characters and storyline.
According to Kishiro, only two episodes were originally planned. At the
time, he was too busy with the manga \"to review the plan coolly\" and
was not serious about an anime adaptation. It remains the only anime
adaptation of *Battle Angel Alita* to date and there are no plans to
revive it.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}28[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-29)^

Main article: [*Alita: Battle

:::

In 2010, [20th Century
Fox](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox"){.mw-redirect} and
director [James Cameron](/wiki/James_Cameron "James Cameron") acquired
the film rights to *Battle
Angel*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}29[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-yahoo-30)^
It was originally brought to Cameron\'s attention by filmmaker
[Guillermo del
Toro](/wiki/Guillermo_del_Toro "Guillermo del Toro").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}30[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-Landau_MTV-31)^
Cameron is said to be a big fan of the manga, and he was waiting until
[CGI
technology](/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery "Computer-generated imagery")
was sufficiently advanced to make a live-action 3D film with effects
comparable to
*[Avatar](/wiki/Avatar_(2009_film) "Avatar (2009 film)")*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}31[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-32)^
The film would be a
[live-action](/wiki/Live-action "Live-action"){.mw-redirect} adaptation
of the first four volumes of the manga series; \"What I'm going to do is
take the spine story and use elements from the first four books. So, the
Motorball from books three and four, and parts of the story of one and
two will all be in the
movie.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}32[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-33)^

*Alita* was originally scheduled to be his next production after the TV
series *[Dark
Angel](/wiki/Dark_Angel_(2000_TV_series) "Dark Angel (2000 TV series)"){.mw-redirect}*,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}33[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-34)^
which was influenced by *Battle Angel
Alita*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}34[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-35)^ After
*Avatar*, he stated he would work on *Avatar* sequels before starting
*Alita*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}35[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-«Alita:_Battle_Angel_after_Avatar_2-36)^

Cameron\'s producer [Jon
Landau](/wiki/Jon_Landau_(film_producer) "Jon Landau (film producer)")
said, \"I am sure you will get to see *Battle Angel*. It is one of my
favourite stories, a great story about a young woman\'s journey to
self-discovery. It is a film that asks the question: What does it mean
to be human? Are you human if you have a heart, a brain or a soul? I
look forward to giving the audience the
film.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}36[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-37)^
Landau half-jokingly stated that the project may be titled *Alita: The
Battle Angel*, because of Cameron\'s tradition in naming his films with
either an \"A\" or a
\"T\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}35[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-«Alita:_Battle_Angel_after_Avatar_2-36)^

In October 2015, it was reported that [Robert
Rodriguez](/wiki/Robert_Rodriguez "Robert Rodriguez") would direct the
film with Cameron and Landau
producing.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}37[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-38)^ On
April 26, 2016, both *[The Hollywood
Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* and
*[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")* reported that
[Maika Monroe](/wiki/Maika_Monroe "Maika Monroe"), [Rosa
Salazar](/wiki/Rosa_Salazar "Rosa Salazar"),
[Zendaya](/wiki/Zendaya "Zendaya") and [Bella
Thorne](/wiki/Bella_Thorne "Bella Thorne") were in the running for the
lead
role.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}38[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-39)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}39[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-40)^
Near the end of May 2016, Salazar was cast as
Alita,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}40[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-41)^ and on
February 7, 2017, *The Hollywood Reporter* reported that [Jennifer
Connelly](/wiki/Jennifer_Connelly "Jennifer Connelly") would be joining
the cast as one of the
villains.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}41[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-42)^

On December 8, 2017, the first trailer for *Battle Angel* was released
to the
public,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}42[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-43)^ and
the film, titled *[Alita: Battle
Angel](/wiki/Alita:_Battle_Angel "Alita: Battle Angel")* and directed by
[Robert Rodriguez](/wiki/Robert_Rodriguez "Robert Rodriguez"), premiered
on February 14, 2019.

*[Gunnm: Martian
Memory](/wiki/Gunnm:_Martian_Memory "Gunnm: Martian Memory")* is an
action [RPG](/wiki/Role-playing_game "Role-playing game") video game for
the [PlayStation](/wiki/PlayStation "PlayStation") by
[Banpresto](/wiki/Banpresto "Banpresto"). It is an adaptation of the
manga, following Alita (Gally) from her discovery in the Zalem dump heap
by Daisuke Ido up through and beyond her career as a TUNED agent. The
story includes additional elements that Kishiro had conceived when he
ended the original manga in 1995, but was unable to implement at the
time, which involved Alita going into outer space. He then expanded the
story, which formed the basis for the manga *[Battle Angel Alita: Last
Order](/wiki/Battle_Angel_Alita:_Last_Order "Battle Angel Alita: Last Order")*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}44[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-45)^

## Reception {#Reception}

[[\[]{.mw-editsection-bracket}[edit](/w/index.php?title=Battle_Angel_Alita&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Reception")[\]]{.mw-editsection-bracket}]{.mw-editsection}
:::

Yukito Kishiro\'s fantasy world garnered widespread acclaim from
critics. *Manga Life* reviewer Adam Volk described the universe as
intricately crafted and deeply engaging, noting that the first volume
alone demonstrated Kishiro\'s mastery of the genre. He highlighted the
manga\'s balance of dynamic action and well-developed, independent
characters---a rarity in comics, films, and television---ultimately
declaring it a classic tale of life\'s
struggles.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}45[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-46)^

Patrick King of *Animefringe* lauded the grandeur of Kishiro\'s
creation, portraying it as a vivid, unsettling, and eerily plausible
vision of humanity\'s future. He observed that the manga explored themes
of human nature and authenticity, distinguishing it from Kishiro\'s more
fantastical [Aqua Knight](/wiki/Aqua_Knight "Aqua Knight") with its
grounded realism. While acknowledging the series\' graphic violence as
unsuitable for younger audiences, King argued that it reinforced the
protagonist\'s
motivations.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}46[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-47)^

Raphael See of [THEM Anime
Reviews](/wiki/THEM_Anime_Reviews "THEM Anime Reviews") regarded it as
one of the finest cyborg-themed anime, praising its seamless integration
of cybernetics into the worldbuilding without overshadowing the
narrative. While he found the series lacking in innovation, its
consistent quality left a strong impression. His sole criticism was its
abrupt ending, which suggested a broader, unexplored
storyline.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}47[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-48)^

Theron Martin of [Anime News
Network](/wiki/Anime_News_Network "Anime News Network") commended
Kishiro\'s meticulous worldbuilding and enduring artistic
prowess,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}48[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-49)^
emphasizing the clarity of the action
sequences.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}49[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-50)^
JapanVisitor.com noted the influence of [Philip K.
Dick](/wiki/Philip_K._Dick "Philip K. Dick") (*[Do Androids Dream of
Electric
Sheep?](/wiki/Do_Androids_Dream_of_Electric_Sheep%3F "Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?")*)
and [Isaac Asimov](/wiki/Isaac_Asimov "Isaac Asimov") (*[I,
Robot](/wiki/I,_Robot "I, Robot")*) on Kishiro\'s
work.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}50[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-51)^
